Hello!

I am a PhD student in Computer Science and Technology at ShanghaiTech University. My research explores how machine learning can turn neuroimaging data into useful representations for understanding and characterizing brain disorders, particularly Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and mild cognitive impairment.

My work spans functional connectivity modeling, graph and meta-learning, language-model-driven representations, and hierarchical learning for medical images. I am interested in how models can use disease-related structure, adapt when labeled data are limited, and remain useful across different datasets. My first-author research has appeared in Medical Image Analysis, IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging, MICCAI, and ISBI.

Alongside academic research, I worked on brain-disease-related medical language models and agents during an internship at United Imaging Intelligence. I also contributed to training DoctorGLM and my research experience includes a 2025–2026 visit at University College London. These experiences inform my interest in connecting methodological research with the practical demands of medical AI.

Research interests

My current interests bring together three related directions:

  • Neuroimaging and Alzheimer’s disease: functional brain networks, mild cognitive impairment, and amyloid-β prediction.
  • Representation learning for brain disorders: graph learning, hierarchical prompts and prototypes, and adaptation with limited data.
  • Medical large language models and agents: domain-specific model training and brain-disease-related applications.

More details are available on the research page.
